Found a baby squirrel?

Most baby squirrels on the ground don't need rescuing. Here's how to tell, and what to do next, one step at a time.

Hurt or in danger? See who to call
  1. 1

    Look before you touch

    Check how old it is. Older babies are often fine on their own.

    Pink, no furEyes shut. Very young.
    Thin fur, stripesEyes shut. Still needs mum.
    Eyes openCrawling, a bit tougher.
    Fluffy tailClimbs and runs. Leave it be.
  2. 2

    Give mum a chance

    Mother squirrels usually come back for a fallen baby. Put it in an open box or basket at the foot of the nearest tree, keep cats, dogs and crows away, and watch quietly from a distance for 1–2 hours.

    Skip straight to step 3 if it's: injured or bleeding · cold · covered in ants or flies · been in a cat's or dog's mouth · still alone at dusk
  3. 3

    Keep it warm, dark and quiet

    Line a small box with a soft cotton cloth (not a towel, claws snag). Put a bottle of warm water wrapped in cloth under one side, so the baby can move away if it gets too hot. Cover the box loosely and keep it somewhere calm.

    Don't feed it or give water yet. A cold baby can't digest anything, and cow's milk can kill it. Warmth is what it needs right now.
  4. 4

    Call a rescuer

    Once it's warm and safe, call one of these. They'll guide you on the phone and can often pick it up.

Until help arrives

No rescue can come for a few hours
  1. Warm it fully first. It should feel warm against your inner wrist.
  2. Rehydrate. 1 tsp salt + 3 tsp sugar in 1 litre warm boiled water. One drop at a time from a 1 mL syringe (no needle).
  3. Hold it upright or belly-down, never on its back. Milk bubbling from the nose means stop.
  4. After a few feeds, move slowly to puppy milk replacer (Lactol, Esbilac) from a vet or pet shop.
  5. After each feed, rub its bottom gently with warm damp cotton until it pees.
2.0 mL
max per feed, about 5% of body weight
Things that harm baby squirrels
  • Cow's, buffalo or human milk
  • Feeding while it's cold, or lying on its back
  • A bath
  • An untreated cat bite. It needs a vet within hours even if it looks fine.
  • Being raised alone as a pet
Can I keep it?

No. Palm squirrels are protected under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972. Caring for it on the way to a rescue is fine. Raising and release should be done by a licensed centre, so it can survive back in the trees.

Donate
  • Give to PfA or ARRC directly. They have the vets, licences and cages, so money goes furthest there.
  • Give supplies: puppy milk replacer, 1 mL syringes, heating pads, cotton cloths. Ask what they're short of.
Running a rescue group

Community

  • One WhatsApp group per area. Pin the steps and numbers. Ask for photo, location pin, condition and time found.
  • Nests come down in tree pruning and monsoon storms. Ask your RWA to check trees for nests before cutting.
  • Put a QR code to this page on park gates and apartment notice boards.

Money

  • Small, specific crowdfunding asks (Milaap, Ketto) with a spending update afterwards.
  • CSR money and tax-deductible donations need a registered trust or society with 12A and 80G.
  • Post a monthly summary: rescued, released, money in and out.

Tools

  • WhatsApp Business quick replies for intake.
  • Google Form into a Sheet, one row per baby.
  • Shared Google Calendar for who's on call.
